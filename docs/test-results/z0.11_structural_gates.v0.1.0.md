## 1. The three rules

Every change is in `pipeline/ozr/gates.py` unless noted. "Corrects" names the PR whose gate the rule replaces or tightens.

| # | Rule | What the code enforces | Closes | Corrects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prose names the use** | For `permitted`, `conditional` or `prohibited` with prose evidence, the quote must contain a taxonomy term (synonym or definition term) of the cell's class on token boundaries. It uses the absence gate's matcher (`_term_in`). Error prefix: `prose names the use:` | Round-2 finding 1 (a second legend, "P Permitted Use SP Special Permit", in a source without a `table_key`) and finding 3 (a second unlisted clause, "Uses not listed also not allowed", supporting `prohibited`). It closes the whole class: a legend line, a footnote, a district heading or an unlisted clause that does not name the use cannot carry a listed status | #11 (legend and unlisted-prose rules), #9 (prose evidence) |
| 2 | **Amendments declare their result** | Amendment records gain `results`: `[{use_class, districts, status, source_id, section, quote}]`. SUPERSEDED fires only for an adopted text amendment whose result covers the cell's class and district, and when either (a) the amendment postdates the cited source, or the date is unknown, or (b) the cell's status contradicts the declared result. The exemption needs the cell's status to equal the declared result, and either the cell cites the result's source and quotes its operative text, or a reviewed `incorporated_in` quote holds. That quote must name a class term, say more than class terms, and be neither a district code nor a table-key symbol. A moratorium never supersedes. A record without a result never supersedes. The old exemption (the cell quote contains the amendment's quote or its reference) is removed | r2a (page title carrying the reference), r2b (operative quote with the wrong status), x1 (`incorporated_in` quote "MR-HI"), g1 (quote "P") and x2 (blank reference matching every quote; the schema now refuses it) | #11 |
| 3 | **Prose-grid scan on the whole ordinance** | The prose-grid check scans every non-GIS source of the jurisdiction (the joined text the absence gate reads), one source at a time, binding headers with that source's own text. A cell exempt under rule 2's declared result is not scanned. Error prefix: `ordinance grid:` | r4 ("Warehousing facilities" in Article 4 for RA): closed, but see the note below | #11 (the cited-source scan) |

**Schema** (`schema/amendment.schema.json`): adds `results`, refuses `results` on any kind other than `adopted_text_amendment` (spec rule 5: moratoria and pending amendments are records, not statuses), and refuses a blank `reference`. **Release gate:** each result's source must be the amendment's source or an `incorporated_in` source. Its quote and section must be found there, the quote must name a class term, and it must not contain the amendment's reference or id. Its class and districts must exist.

**Plumbing.** `table_errors`, `cell_errors` and `labels.label_errors` take the jurisdiction text and the amendments, and `labels.gate_cells` loads `amendments.json`. So `regate`, `grade` and `release` apply identical rules.

**How r4 is actually closed.** r4 now gets two errors, and neither comes from the Article 3 row the review pointed at:
- Rule 1 fires because "Warehousing facilities" has no `warehouse_distribution` term: the taxonomy term "warehouse" does not match "Warehousing".
- Rule 3 fires because the Article 3 grid places "1. Electrical substation distribution centers and transformer stations" under RA. That row matches the term "distribution center", so this is the broad class-term row match deferred in Z0.10, not the warehousing row.

The review's row, "5. a. Warehousing including Self-Storage Facilities", contains no taxonomy term either, so rule 3 does not select it. The synthetic tests prove rule 3's mechanics: the per-source scan, the cross-source alias refusal and the declared-result exemption.

**Residual.** Prose that names the use with a taxonomy term, for example "Warehouses are permitted in RA", would still not be checked against a blank use-table row whose label names the use without a taxonomy term ("Warehousing ..."). Closing that needs a row matcher wider than the taxonomy terms, which is the same maintainer decision as narrowing the current one.

On the real data, rule 3 errors are live in the ledgers: 10 Douglas grok labels and 6 Fauquier extractor cells, all rejected before Z0.11 by the cited-source scan.

### Special cases kept, and why

They are not clearly superseded:
- **Table-key overlap.** It still guards absence evidence. A prose quote can also name the use and overlap a key line.
- **Unlisted-clause rule.** A quote can name the use and still be the unlisted clause.

The round-2 residuals x5 and g4, about prose-grid placement semantics, were not in scope and are unchanged. Widening the scan to the whole ordinance gives them more text to act on. Neither withheld a published cell in this run.

## 4. Disputed share, two ways

`ozr measure` now also reports `disputed_share_counting_rejected` and `disputed_share_counting_rejected_differing` (`pipeline/ozr/measure.py`), overall, by jurisdiction and by family. No gate reads them. As before, the plan-gate definition counts a key as disputed only when both reviewer labels passed the gates and differ.
