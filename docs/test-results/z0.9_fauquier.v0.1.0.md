**Date:** 2026-10-11 · **Branch:** `feat/z0.9-fauquier`, stacked on `feat/z0.8-gate-fixes` (31e44b4) · **Covers:** registering the manually fetched Fauquier ordinance, a deterministic OCR layer for image-only PDF pages, extraction, blind review, grading and measurement for us-51061, and the rebuilt v0.1.0 release.

## TL;DR

- **Fauquier is in.** 45 of its 161 cells are published (prohibited 35, conditional 6, unlisted 4) and 116 are withheld with the gate and run id. The release now holds 595 cells over six jurisdictions. `ozr release` exits 0, and the consumer contract `zoning-atlas.release/0.1` reports 0 errors, both in `ozr.contract` and in the read-only reference implementation. The 550 cells of the other five jurisdictions are byte-for-byte unchanged.
- **OCR works, but it publishes nothing for Fauquier yet.** Three image-only pages were OCR'd: Article 3 pages 9 and 10 (the scanned use-table pages) and Article 4 page 98. 5 extractor cells passed the gates on OCR evidence. None is published, because no reviewer label passed for those keys. Most use-table rows on page 10 fail the quote gate because tesseract misreads their labels, for example "Petrokum oroducts bulk stoage". The gate is meant to reject such cells.
- **The one Fauquier known answer is unscored.** The extractor read BP `datacenter` as `permitted` from the page image, which is the expected status. Its cell failed three gates: the OCR row label is misread ("Centerusmg"), the cited symbol `P25` (P with footnote 25) is not in the table key, and the grid cannot place the row.
- **Reviewer agreement is weak.** codex and grok both passed a label on only 16 cells and agree on 2. Most disagreements are `lp_gas`, where codex says `unlisted` and grok mostly says `prohibited`. Fauquier's disputed share is 0.087 (14 / 161), inside the 0.15 gate.
- **Cost of the clean pass:** $4.40 for Claude extraction and $4.73 for the grok review, $9.14 priced in total, plus 936,291 codex tokens that are not priced. Summed model wall time was 1.76 h.

## 2. OCR layer

**Engine.** The tesseract CLI, with the engine stamped as the first line of every OCR layer:

```text
[ocr engine: tesseract 5.5.3;  leptonica-1.87.0; eng.traineddata sha256=daa0c97d651c19fba3b25e81317cd697e9908c8208090c94c3905381c23fc047; pdfium=156.0.8076.0; render=300dpi; threshold=160/170; psm=6,7; OMP_THREAD_LIMIT=1; --oem 3 -l eng]
```

It runs from the system package `tesseract 5.5.3-1` with `tesseract-data-eng 2:4.1.0-5`. No Python dependency was added: numpy and Pillow were already installed as transitive dependencies.

**Which pages are OCR'd.** A page is image-only when it embeds at least one image and pdfplumber finds at most 20 non-whitespace characters on it. The scanned pages carry only a printed page number such as "III - 9". Over the ten Fauquier PDFs, that rule selects exactly these pages:

| Source | Page | What it is | Grid | Rows | Non-empty cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| us-51061-zo-art3 | 9 | Use table: business services (3-313) and motor vehicle uses (3-314) | 20 columns | 42 | 279 |
| us-51061-zo-art3 | 10 | Use table: wholesaling (3-315), limited industrial (3-316) and industrial (3-317), including the data center row | 20 columns | 37 | 206 |
| us-51061-zo-art4 | 98 | A scanned figure | ruled box | 4 | 11 (noise) |

Article 4 page 88 (197 characters of text plus a map) is correctly not selected. Pages 5, 6, 12 and 16 of Article 3 have a text layer with letter-spaced glyphs ("S ee P ag e"). They are outside the OCR rule, and their row labels rarely survive the quote gate. That is a known limitation, not handled in this step.

**Method (`pipeline/ozr/ocr.py`):**

1. Render the page at 300 dpi with pdfium. The embedded scans are about 141 dpi, so this upsamples them.
2. Find ruling lines from the raster. A pixel row whose longest dark run reaches 40% of the width is a horizontal rule. Within the table band, a pixel column whose longest dark run reaches 30% of the band height is a vertical rule.
3. OCR each cell separately. Row labels are scaled 2x and read with `--psm 6`. Symbol cells are scaled 3x, binarised and read with `--psm 7`.
4. Write the cells as `a | b | c` rows, the same shape as the PDF table layer.

**Engine choice.** `--oem 1` was tried first. On page 10 it read single `P` symbols as `:` in five cells. `--oem 3`, tesseract's default LSTM engine, reads them as `P`, matching the page image. Its output is byte-identical to a run with no `--oem` flag.

**Determinism.** Page 10 was OCR'd in two separate processes, once by `ozr segment` and once by a standalone run of the same code. The outputs are byte-identical. Text hashes (`segments.json`): art3 `a517e7cd...3d30`, art4 `087767c3...1253`.

**Layers.** Each source text now has up to three layers: the raw PDF text, the reconstructed table text, and the OCR text. The OCR text comes last, under its own marker, and holds one `[page N]` block per page. `gates.text_layers` splits the three in one place.

- A quote, label or section must match inside a single layer; a match across a boundary is rejected.
- The table-grid gate uses the PDF table layer first. Only if that layer cannot place the row and column does it use the OCR grid. The two grids are never mixed.
- Each passing cell's `matched_text` records the layer of every field. Table cells also record `symbol`, the layer whose grid supplied the symbol.

**Grade cap (spec delta).** A cell is capped at grade C when any of its evidence fields matched only in the OCR text: quote, row label, column header, section, definition, or the grid that supplied the symbol. OCR can misread, and the quote gate cannot tell a misread from the law. `ozr grade` applies the cap, and the release gate rejects an OCR-only cell graded above C.

**Grid caveat.** The page-10 header row is OCR'd as `C1`, `C2`, `Cv` and `1-2`. Under the existing Z0.8 grid rule, a row binds to the nearest earlier header row that holds the column. The 5 OCR cells in the C-1, R-1, R-2, RA and RC columns therefore bound to page 9's header, which OCR'd `R-1`, `R-2` and `C-1` correctly. The two pages share one 20-column layout, so the columns are right. But binding a row to a header on another page is a property of the existing grid rule, and it applies to the PDF table layer too.

## 6. Limitations and follow-ups

1. **OCR misreads keep most of the scanned table out.** The extractor and codex read the page images correctly, but the quote gate checks the OCR text. Better OCR, or a human transcription of the two pages recorded as its own layer, would recover the industrial rows on page 10: data center, warehouse and distribution center, heavy and general industrial, and petroleum bulk storage.
2. **Footnoted symbols** such as `P25` and combined symbols such as `SP/SE` and `A/SE` are not in the quoted key. Mapping them needs a maintainer decision: a footnote rule, or key entries with quotes.
3. **Letter-spaced text** on Article 3 pages 5, 6, 12 and 16 breaks row labels in the raw layer, and those pages have no rulings to rebuild a table from.
4. **Reviewer disagreement on `lp_gas`**: unlisted versus prohibited, discussed above.
5. **Absence evidence over OCR pages is only as complete as the OCR.** The absence gate scans every layer for class terms, and an OCR misread hides an occurrence: page 10 reads "Data Centerusmg", "Distnbution Centeron" and "General Industnal". Two published unlisted cells are in a class whose term sits on a scanned page: us-51061/M-G and M-R `datacenter` (both disputed, grade C). The hidden occurrence is the Article 3 "Data Center" row, which has an entry only in the BP column. The extractor's explanations for the other data center occurrences (Sec. 3-200, footnote 25, Sec. 4-603, Sec. 4-605) cover the same point, and M-G and M-R are Marshall districts under the Sec. 4-1002 table. So the cells are not wrong on that account, but the gate did not check that occurrence.
6. **Rows can bind to a header on another page**, a property of the Z0.8 grid rule. See the grid caveat in section 2.
